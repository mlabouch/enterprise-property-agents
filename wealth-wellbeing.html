Wealth and Wellbeing for Women

Investors and home seekers — at every stage of the journey.

What it’s all about

Time is the thing nobody has enough of

Many young professional women are realising that their first home or apartment can, and should, be a solo adventure. Like most of us they are busy creating careers — in law, medicine, accounting, or building businesses across a myriad of other professions. Between that and a busy social life, the time left to look after themselves and their future is limited.

Younger families face the same squeeze. One partner is extremely busy earning that essential good living; the other is busy with the family and simply getting on with the home. One or both are looking ahead to building the family’s future, with property investment as the target — and again, the time to act on it is limited.

And then there are women coming into the later parts of their lives, perhaps with a big house somewhere, too much space, or ongoing maintenance that has become an issue. Having the skills, time and energy to get the old home ready for market while simultaneously finding something more appropriate for the future is a challenge, to say the least.

Invaluable Our support has proven so for many of these clients

How that support actually works

  • We build your understanding of the markets, and of the search and acquisition process — essential for the “newbie” purchaser and the not-so-newbie owner occupier alike.
  • We help with structures and funding, and introductions to the other experts you’ll need to succeed.
  • We look for, find and secure the downsized special house, the first dream home, or the first investment that sets a portfolio on its way.

Upsizing

“Help!” — which is where we come in

Most of us will own and live in a number of properties over a lifetime. We start out with the bare minimum: an inner-suburban flat or apartment, or a modest home further out. Then the upward journey begins — a better location, a larger dwelling for a growing family, closer to work or to the right schools. Bigger and better as lifestyle and finances improve.

The pressure to find that new home often falls on the shoulders of the partner who isn’t working full time. Stuck on the computer, nights and weekends, trawling property websites and trying to get to the truth behind a selling agent’s often outrageous claims about their particular listing “this week”.

Add trying to work out the best structure to buy within, sourcing the best funding, and finding introductions to the other experts you desperately need — and it’s a lot. We are superbly placed to assist with all of it, then to investigate the market and help you find and secure that next dream home or first investment.

Divestment and downsizing

When the family home has become too much house

Often the same partner carries responsibility for the next step too: selling the family home and downsizing. Sometimes it follows a family breakup, or the passing of one of the partnership, which can leave a surviving widow in a difficult position.

The children have long gone. The large home is now surplus to needs and demands upkeep and ongoing maintenance that the money may not stretch to. The surviving partner may have been the family carer for decades, with limited scope to compete in today’s employment market. What to do, and how to do it, sits front of mind.

Working that out is where we come in — bringing in the teams of expert advisors and planners to help plan the immediate steps of asset divestment, repositioning and reinvestment.

We make sure the old home is genuinely saleable and positioned to achieve the best price, acting as your Vendor’s Advocate if that’s the plan. Then we pull together the search-find-secure program to get you somewhere more appropriate to live, without the headaches of the house you’ve left behind.

Many surviving partners are understandably inexperienced in tricky property markets. It is often a difficult time, and cutting through a selling agent’s claims is the last thing anyone needs. Structures, funding and expert introductions can be simply too much on top of everything else. We are well placed to carry that load.
